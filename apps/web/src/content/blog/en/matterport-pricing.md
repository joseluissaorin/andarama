**Matterport pricing in 2026** is no longer a flat price per plan: it depends on how many **active spaces** you want live at once. ## How Matterport calculates the price

Matterport doesn't sell software; it sells cloud hosting for "spaces" (the scan of a house, an office, a store). What you pay depends on:

1. **The plan**, which sets the range of active spaces, the number of users and the features.
2. **The number of active spaces** within that range. The price climbs in steps.
3. **Billing**: month to month or yearly, with up to 16% off for paying the year up front.
4. **Per-space add-ons**: schematic floor plans, MatterPak files, reactivating archived spaces.
5. **The camera**: a phone and several consumer 360 cameras (Ricoh Theta, Insta360) work from Starter up; full compatibility, including Matterport's Pro cameras and LiDAR scanners, starts at Professional.

There are steps in between (Starter at 15; Professional at 25, 30, 40, 75, 100 and 125; Business at 125, 150 and 200). They follow the same slope, a little under $3 per space per month.

**Per-space add-ons**, also from Matterport's page: schematic floor plans from $14.99 (more for 24-hour or 6-hour turnaround), MatterPak technical files at $49 to $59 depending on the plan, and $9.99 to reactivate an archived space beyond the reactivations your plan includes.

The honest read: at 2026 prices, **Matterport Starter is not expensive** for someone with a handful of listings. Ten spaces cost $288 a year. What makes Matterport expensive is growing (Professional for pro cameras, Business for teams) and, above all, the one thing you can't do: take the tour with you.

## The cost that isn't on the price table: lock-in

With Matterport, the interactive tour (the one you walk through with the mouse, with the dollhouse and measurements) **only works while it is hosted on Matterport**. Drop to a smaller plan and the spaces that don't fit get archived; if Matterport changes its prices, you accept them or lose the work. What you can buy is a data export (the 3D model, the point cloud, floor plans). That is useful to an architect, but it is not the tour you embed on a website.

For a brokerage that turns over listings every few months this barely matters: the space gets archived when the home sells. For a museum, a university or a company that wants the tour for years, it is the main cost, and it doesn't show up on any invoice until the day you want to leave.

## When Matterport is worth the money

To be fair, there are cases where Matterport is the right call:

- **Digital twins with measurements**: remodeling, architecture, inspections, insurance. 360 photos don't give you a measurable 3D model.
- **High-volume real estate** on portals and MLS workflows that already expect a Matterport link, with enough turnover that the active-space cap doesn't bite.
- **The dollhouse view** as a selling point for luxury listings.

If that is you, Matterport is the benchmark and everything else is a substitute.
