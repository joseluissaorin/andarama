For a **real estate virtual tour**, the right 360 camera is the one that takes clean 8K photos with HDR, fires from your phone and fits in an agent's pocket. Today that means the mid-range (Insta360 X4 or X5, Ricoh Theta X). The high end, with larger sensors (Ricoh Theta Z1), handles dark interiors better and is what professional photographers pick. Entry-level cameras and phones are fine for experimenting, not for showing a home to a buyer. ## What actually matters (and what doesn't)

Manufacturers sell video: 8K, stabilization, slow motion. For real estate almost none of that counts. Here's what does:

| Criterion | Why it matters | What to look for |
|---|---|---|
| **Photo resolution** | Buyers zoom in; below 8K the image looks soft | Photos of at least 8192 × 4096 (about 30 megapixels after stitching) |
| **HDR or bracketing** | Living room with a window: either the window blows out or the room goes dark | Automatic photo HDR, or bracketing merged in the app |
| **Sensor size** | Dark interiors, noise | Bigger is better; 1-inch sensors are the high end |
| **Remote-shooting app** | You need to get out of the shot | Shoot from your phone with live preview and a timer |
| **Clean automatic stitching** | Seams at eye level give the tour away | In-camera or in-app stitching with no manual work |
| **Equirectangular export** | It's the tour format | 2:1 JPG with metadata; RAW (DNG) is a pro bonus |
| **Battery life and startup** | Ten listings in a morning | A full day's battery and startup in seconds |

What doesn't matter: video resolution, waterproofing, stabilization and creative modes. If your marketing team will also use the camera for video, those count; for tours, they don't.

## The models, by tier

Prices change often and vary by retailer, so we give rough bands; check current prices before you buy.

### Mid-range: the sweet spot

- **Insta360 X4 and X5**: 8K photos (roughly 70 megapixels), HDR, excellent stitching, and a very polished app with remote shooting and direct export of the equirectangular image to your camera roll. It's what most brokerages that shoot their own tours carry. The X5 improves the sensor and the lenses (which are replaceable) over the X4. Price band: a few hundred dollars.
- **Ricoh Theta X**: photos up to 11K, a touchscreen on the camera itself (no phone needed to shoot) and good HDR. Less of an action camera, more of a work tool.

### High end: for photographers and tricky interiors

- **Ricoh Theta Z1**: two 1-inch sensors, RAW in DNG, very clean 7K photos with little noise. It's the benchmark among real estate photographers for interior image quality, in exchange for a lower nominal resolution and a higher price (around a thousand dollars). Stitching leaves a very small nadir.

### Entry level and phones

- **Older or used 360 cameras** (Insta360 One X2, first-generation GoPro Max, Theta SC2): they work, and 5.7K to 6K photos are enough to get started, but it shows when you zoom in.
- **A phone with a spherical panorama app**: for testing the workflow, not for clients. We explain why in [how to take a 360 photo with an iPhone](/en/blog/360-photo-iphone/).

## Accessories you actually need

- **A thin monopod with a base**, 5 to 6.5 feet tall: the camera "erases" it during stitching because it sits in the blind spot between the two lenses. A regular tripod leaves its legs on the floor. If anything remains, a **nadir patch** with your brokerage's logo covers it; Andarama applies it in the media library without touching the original.
- **Spare batteries** and a fast memory card.
- **A bubble level** on the monopod, or the leveling in the app: a tilted photo can be fixed later (Andarama has horizon leveling), but it's better to avoid it.

## How to shoot a listing in twenty minutes

1. **Prep the home**: lights on, blinds up, interior doors open, personal items put away, toilet lid down. A 360 photo shows everything.
2. **Height**: camera at about 5 feet; that's eye level, and it's what makes the viewer feel they're standing in the room. Not on the table, not on the floor.
3. **Position**: the center of each room, or two spots in large and L-shaped rooms; one at the front door and one on the deck, patio or balcony.
4. **HDR on** for any room with windows.
5. **Get out of the shot**: fire from your phone with a timer while you stand behind a door or in the hallway.
6. **Name the photos** by room in the app if you have time; if not, Andarama's importer renames them by pattern afterward.
7. **Export** the equirectangular JPGs to your phone or computer.

## Buy or hire out?

A mid-range camera pays for itself on the first listing if agents shoot their own tours: twenty minutes of photos versus a photographer's per-shoot fee. A professional photographer with a Theta Z1 still makes sense for new construction, luxury homes and large commercial spaces, where image quality and lighting count and clients notice. Many brokerages do both: the office camera for everyday listings and the pro for the showcase properties.
