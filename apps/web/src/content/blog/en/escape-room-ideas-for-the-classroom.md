A **classroom escape room** works when the puzzle requires the subject content and the story gives it a reason. Everything else (locks, timers, hidden objects) is packaging. Here are twelve ideas ready to build in 360, organized by subject, each with its story, its locks and the photos it needs. They all use the same building blocks: hidden treasures, gated questions, variables that open doors and clues scattered through the scenes. How to put those pieces together, step by step, is in [how to build a digital escape room in 360](/en/blog/digital-escape-room/).

## How to read each idea

Each idea lists **the story** (what you tell the class), **the locks** (the puzzles that open each room and the content they require) and **the photos** (whether you can shoot them at school or need openly licensed panoramas). Grade levels are suggestions.

## Science

### 1. The contaminated lab (Chemistry, grades 9-10)

**Story.** A reagent has spilled in the lab and the ventilation system has locked down. To open the door, students must identify the substance and neutralize it.
**Locks.** Read the label on a bottle (image hotspot) and calculate molarity; pick the correct hazard pictogram; put the steps of the neutralization procedure in order with a multiple-answer question.
**Photos.** Your school's chem lab, with bottles labeled on purpose for the shot.

### 2. The blackout (Physics and engineering, grades 10-12)

**Story.** A cascading power outage. Each room is part of the building's systems (electrical panel, mechanical room, shop) and circuits must be restored in order.
**Locks.** Calculate the equivalent resistance of the diagram taped to the wall; choose the right fuse for the current; read an energy-use graph. A **Counter/state** hotspot "turns on the lights" in each room after a correct answer.
**Photos.** The school shop and mechanical room. If you can't get in, panoramas from a science and technology museum.

### 3. Outbreak at the field hospital (Biology, grades 9-11)

**Story.** A disease is spreading and the team must find the pathogen, how it spreads and the treatment before the next patients arrive.
**Locks.** Tell a virus from a bacterium in a microscope image; read a symptom table to rule out hypotheses; calculate a dose using proportions.
**Photos.** A classroom turned into a "hospital" with cots and signs; the class handles the set design.

## Math

### 4. The bank vault (Math, grades 6-7)

**Story.** A classic breakout: the vault combination is split into four digits hidden around the office.
**Locks.** Each digit comes from solving a problem: fractions, exponents, a ratio problem, the mean of a data set. The last quiz asks for the full combination.
**Photos.** The front office or the principal's office, with permission; or any office.

### 5. The explorer's route (Geometry and geography, grades 7-8)

**Story.** An old map with the coordinates erased. To know where to go, students must calculate distances and angles.
**Locks.** The Pythagorean theorem on the plan of a town square; map scale (zoomable image hotspot); latitude and longitude using the tour's map mode.
**Photos.** Openly licensed panoramas of a plaza or an archaeological site. Andarama lets you place each scene on OpenStreetMap, and visitors see it on the viewer's map.

## English Language Arts and world languages

### 6. The missing manuscript (ELA, grades 9-12)

**Story.** A Shakespeare first edition has vanished from the library, and the clues are hidden in the texts themselves.
**Locks.** Identify the author of a passage by its style (PDF hotspot); scan a line of iambic pentameter; name a literary device; put the acts of a play in order with a multiple-answer question.
**Photos.** Your school library, or a historic library from Wikimedia Commons.

### 7. Lost in translation (World languages, any level)

**Story.** The whole tour is in the target language: notes, audio, questions. Escaping requires understanding.
**Locks.** A voicemail (audio hotspot) students must pull a time from; a note with a grammar mistake that opens the lock once corrected; a short video with an instruction. Andarama translates scenes and hotspots by language, so the same escape room can have a version in two languages.
**Photos.** Any space in your school.

## History and art

### 8. Night at the museum (AP Art History)

**Story.** The artworks have been moved to the wrong galleries and must go back before the museum opens.
**Locks.** Identify the style of a work in a zoomable image hotspot; date it with a single-answer question; match work and artist; a **Comparison** slider of two images hides the detail that gives away the forgery.
**Photos.** Openly licensed museum panoramas (Wikimedia Commons has many galleries from major museums) or your local museum, with permission.

### 9. The fort under siege (U.S. History, grades 5-8)

**Story.** A Revolutionary War fort is surrounded and the garrison must find the way out using clues from soldiers' letters and journals.
**Locks.** Identify the parts of the fort (floating labels students have to uncover); put key events of the war in order; read an excerpt from an adapted primary source.
**Photos.** Historic forts and battlefields have openly licensed panoramas in many states; a trip to the nearest historic site with a 360 camera also works, and gives you material for a [virtual field trip](/en/blog/virtual-field-trips/) later.

## Other areas

### 10. Locked in on day one (Advisory and freshman orientation)

**Story.** New students "got locked in" on the first day and have to find the way out by exploring the building: front office, library, gym, science lab.
**Locks.** Questions about rules, bell schedules and who's who, with the answers hidden in the scenes themselves (a poster, the staff photo).
**Photos.** The whole school, in one morning with a 360 camera. The same tour becomes your virtual tour for open house and back-to-school night.

### 11. Kitchen inspection (Culinary arts CTE and health)

**Story.** Surprise inspection: the team must fix every food safety violation before the health inspector walks in.
**Locks.** Find the five hidden violations (treasures: the rag on the cutting board, the open fridge, the expired date); pick the correct holding temperature; order the cleaning and sanitizing steps.
**Photos.** The culinary program kitchen or the cafeteria kitchen.

### 12. The control room (Workplace safety, CTE and corporate training)

**Story.** Emergency drill: an alarm on the shop floor, and the team must find the exits, the fire extinguishers and the assembly point, and decide what to do in each situation.
**Locks.** Find the extinguishers (treasures) in each scene; a question on fire classes and the right extinguisher; choose the evacuation route on the floor plan.
**Photos.** The real shop or plant floor. This is also the format we suggest for [workplace safety training](/en/training/), with a PDF certificate at the end.
