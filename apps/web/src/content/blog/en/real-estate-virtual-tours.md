A **real estate virtual tour** that actually helps sell a home isn't a row of 360 photos. It's a walkthrough that follows the real showing (entry, living room, kitchen, bedrooms, baths, backyard), with a floor plan so buyers know where they are, short captions where they matter (square footage, exposure, recent updates), and a clean link in the MLS listing and on your website. With a mid-range 360 camera you can shoot a home in twenty minutes and build the tour in half an hour, and the software costs anywhere from nothing to a few dollars a month. ## What it's good for, in practice

- **Screening showings**: a buyer who has walked through the home in 360 and still asks for a showing is serious. Fewer wasted showings for you and the seller.
- **Out-of-town buyers**: relocations, second homes, buyers moving across the country. The tour replaces the first visit.
- **Rentals and new construction** with a model unit: show it once, lease or sell it many times.
- **Winning listings**: a seller who sees that their home will get a virtual tour is more likely to sign with you.

## What to shoot and how

One 360 photo per room, two in large or L-shaped rooms, and one at the entry. A three-bedroom home is eight to ten photos. A few habits that save reshoots:

- **Camera at about 5 feet** on a thin monopod. That's eye level, and it's what makes the viewer feel like they're standing in the room.
- **Lights on, blinds open.** The camera's HDR handles bright windows.
- **Get out of the shot.** Trigger the camera from your phone and step behind a door.
- **Declutter and remove personal items.** A 360 photo shows everything, including what's behind you.
- **Shoot from the middle of the room**, not a corner, so the viewer turns and sees all four walls at a similar distance.
- **The nadir**: a thin monopod mostly disappears in the stitching. If the base still shows, a nadir patch with your brokerage's logo covers it.

For which camera to buy and the settings we use, see our guide to the [best 360 camera for real estate](/en/blog/best-360-camera-for-real-estate/).

## Building the tour in half an hour

1. **Import**: upload the whole batch. The importer sorts files by name, lets you rename by pattern ("Living room", "Kitchen") and creates all the scenes at once, optionally linked in sequence.
2. **Floor plan**: if you have the plan (the one from the listing works), add it and place each scene with a click. Buyers see a minimap with where they are and which way they're facing. In real estate that matters more than any visual effect.
3. **Navigation**: in the graph, every arrow is a doorway. With "follow the path", buyers enter each room facing forward, like walking through. Two minutes per home.
4. **Starting view** for each scene: the one that shows the room at its best (the living room window, not the door).
5. **Captions**: one short text hotspot per room with square footage, exposure and what matters ("renovated 2024", "walk-in closet"). A link hotspot to your phone or email on the first scene, and a contact form if you want leads straight from the tour.
6. **Link preview**: in the tour settings, set the title, description and image that show up when the link is pasted into a text message or email. It's the first thing the buyer sees.
7. **Publish**: link, QR code (for the yard sign or the window) and embed code. Unlisted if the seller doesn't want it indexed, with a password or an expiry date if needed.

## Publishing on the MLS, portals and your website

Most MLS systems have a **virtual tour URL** field, and listing portals accept a tour link: paste the address of the published tour and you're done. Many MLSs require that link to be **unbranded** (no agent name, phone number or logo), so keep a clean version for that field and check your MLS rules. On your own website, the embed code or web component drops the tour into the listing page; on WordPress, [our plugin](/en/wordpress/) does it with a block or a shortcode. On the Excursión plan and above, the tour is also served without the Andarama badge and on your own domain (`tours.yourbrokerage.com`), which is what a brokerage with its own brand expects.

- **Luxury and new construction**, where the wow factor matters and the budget allows it.

## Mistakes we see every day

- **Camera on the floor or on a table.** The home looks like it was shot by a toddler or a giant.
- **No floor plan.** Buyers are lost by the second room.
- **Long captions.** Nobody reads three paragraphs in a tour. Square footage and exposure, that's it.
- **The same starting view in every scene.** Every room has a good side.
- **Tours published under the vendor's brand and domain.** Buyers remember the vendor, not you.
- **Tours that vanish** when you switch providers. With Andarama you can export them as a package and the software is open source: your archive of tours belongs to you.
